Introduction to Renal Physiology

The kidneys are the primary organs for maintaining the internal environment (homeostasis). For a Biochemistry student, the kidney is viewed as a complex biological filter and endocrine organ.

Ultrafiltration

Occurs at the glomerulus where blood is filtered under pressure.

Tubular Reabsorption

Essential solutes like glucose and amino acids are reclaimed.

Tubular Secretion

Waste products and ions ($H^+$, $K^+$) are actively secreted.

2

Assessment of Glomerular Function

Glomerular function is assessed by measuring the Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR).

A. Non-Protein Nitrogenous (NPN) Compounds

Serum Creatinine

  • Biochemistry: Derived from Creatine Phosphate in muscle metabolism.
  • Clinical Significance: Produced at a constant rate proportional to muscle mass. Freely filtered and not reabsorbed.
  • The Inverse Relationship: If GFR decreases by 50%, serum creatinine roughly doubles.

Blood Urea Nitrogen (BUN)

  • Biochemistry: Urea is the end product of protein catabolism, synthesized in the Liver via the Urea Cycle.
  • Limitation: Affected by diet, dehydration, and tissue breakdown.
  • BUN/Creatinine Ratio: High ratios (>20:1) suggest Pre-renal causes like dehydration.

B. Clearance Tests

Clearance Formula

$$Clearance (C) = \frac{U \times V}{P}$$
$U$: Urine Conc.
$V$: Urine Volume/min
$P$: Plasma Conc.
3

Assessment of Tubular Function

Specific Gravity & Osmolality

Measures the kidney's power to concentrate urine. A fixed gravity of 1.010 (Isosthenuria) indicates severe tubular failure.

Electrolyte Regulation

Kidneys regulate $Na^+$ (via Aldosterone) and $K^+$. In failure, Hyperkalemia becomes a clinical emergency.

4

Proteinuria: The Clinical "Leak"

The healthy glomerular basement membrane is negatively charged, repelling albumin.

Microalbuminuria

$30–300 \text{ mg/day}$. Early sign of Diabetic Nephropathy.

Nephrotic Syndrome

$>3.5 \text{ g/day}$. Massive protein loss and edema.

5

Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)

Stage eGFR ($mL/min$) Description
1$\geq 90$Damage with normal function
2$60–89$Mild decrease in function
3$30–59$Clinical Tipping Point
4$15–29$Severe decrease in function
5$< 15$Kidney Failure / ESRD

Summary for Students

  • Assess Filtration: Look at eGFR and Creatinine.
  • Assess Early Damage: Look at uACR (Albuminuria).
  • Assess Tubular Integrity: Look at Specific Gravity.
  • Assess Balance: Look at Bicarbonate ($HCO_3^-$).
